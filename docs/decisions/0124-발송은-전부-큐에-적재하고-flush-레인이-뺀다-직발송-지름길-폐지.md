# ADR-0124: Every send is loaded into the queue and the flush lane takes it out (the direct-send shortcut is abolished)

- 상태: **폐기 (Superseded by ADR-0125)** — discard reason: to get the order, it gave up `delivered` in the response, but **a synchronous drain could have had both.** The reason this ADR gave when it put that option under 「거부한 대안」 ("the sender pays for someone else's backlog") was a cost worth paying at this scale (cap 100 per recipient · human-conversation message rates). **It was discarded before code started — implementation loss 0.** The gains of abolishing direct send and not needing a seat reservation are inherited by ADR-0125 as they are. ~~확정 (2026-08-01, basis: user decision — "오는건 한군데일거아냐? 그럼 큐에 적재하고 뺄때 한꺼번에 idle로 바꾸고 빼면 되는거 아님?", "ㅇㅇ 그렇게 가자")~~

## 맥락

While implementing ADR-0121 decision 2 ("keep the order") we ran into **the problem of the direct-send shortcut and the queue competing**.

In the **structure before this decision** there were two delivery paths (the §영향 "There is one send path" below is the state after this decision — this paragraph is history). If the queue in front of the recipient is empty and it can receive, **the sending thread writes to the other side's stdin on the spot and answers `delivered`** (direct send). If there is something in the queue or the other side is mid-turn, it piles up behind and answers `pending`. The reason direct send existed was not performance but the **response meaning** — if everything goes to the queue, every send is `pending`, so the sender cannot tell whether it was actually delivered.

The problem comes from the fact that the decision and the injection cannot fit in one critical section. Queue manipulation is inside a single `Mutex<MessagingState>`, and `DeliveryPort::inject` must be called **with the lock released** (lock discipline — making an external call while holding a lock risks order inversion or deadlock with the locks the other side takes). So a window opens between the "is the queue empty" decision and the actual write, and **two sends that arrive almost at the same time both see "empty" and both take the direct send.** Two things blew up here:

1. **Isolation — a letter is trapped in the queue.** The one that came first fails while writing and is pushed to the queue, but nobody rings the doorbell that would open that queue (by design, to avoid repeating re-injection into a side that just broke, it deliberately does not press its own doorbell). It is trapped until the next occasion arrives. **★This is a defect of the pre-seat version, and in the current HEAD the seat reservation has already closed it★** — the gap was closed by the joining send's doorbell and the deferred re-notification, which flush the batch, and the closing assertion of `a_direct_injection_holds_a_seat_so_a_concurrent_send_cannot_overtake_it` (`parked_len == 0`, "no isolation") seals that. That is, **it is not a bug alive now, but a property that opens again if the seat is removed** — the new structure must necessarily provide this property again (§영향 unverified ① and ② are the items to confirm it).
2. **Order — a later one can be delivered first.** But both are new letters that just arrived, and this is a different matter from "a new one jumps ahead of an old one" that ADR-0121 dealt with.

The 2026-07-31 implementation (`76ea887`, wip) **reserved a seat for direct send too** to block 2 (`reserve_direct_seat` in the same lock span as the decision, restored to the reserved turn on injection failure). It works but has a cost — while a seat is held, a concurrent send gets the "there is one ahead" decision and is parked, so for 100 concurrent sends to one recipient, what used to be 100 `delivered` becomes **1 `delivered` + 99 `pending`**. And the devices where direct send, seat and joining decisions refer to each other kept growing.

**The user's remark (2026-08-01):** "결국 간단하게 구현하면 되는데 왜 이렇게 복잡하게 끌고 갔냐 덕분에 코드만 복잡해졌잖아." And an alternative: "오는 건 한 군데일 거 아냐? 그럼 큐에 적재하고 뺄 때 한꺼번에 빼면 되는 거 아님?"

## 결정

**1. Abolish the direct-send shortcut. Every send is loaded into the queue, and the flush lane takes it out.**

After finishing the per-recipient decision, the send entry point **puts it into the queue inside the lock and ends.** The actual injection is performed by the flush lane in a batch, oldest first. The order guarantee holds naturally by the **order of loading inside the lock**, with no separate device — devices like seat reservation and direct-send joining decisions become unnecessary.

**2. `delivered` disappears from the send response vocabulary. The answer for a normal send is `pending` alone.**

The per-recipient result becomes two: `pending` (accepted, it will go) or `failed` (rejected). **The `failed` branches stay as they are** — they are decided *before* putting into the queue, so they can answer immediately: no such name (`RECIPIENT_NOT_FOUND`) · ambiguous same name (`RECIPIENT_AMBIGUOUS`) · mailbox exceeded (`MAILBOX_FULL`) · **reply contract exceeded (`REQUEST_CAPACITY`)**. That is, **typos and nonexistent recipients are still reported immediately.** What is lost is only the distinction between "went in right now" and "is standing in line".

**3. The `delivered` terminal vocabulary and meaning of the ledger stay as they are. But the initial state becomes uniformly `pending`.** `delivered` = "written to stdin and fully accepted" is recorded at the **actual injection moment** (ADR-0104 · ADR-0121 decision 3 kept). What changes is ① the **return vocabulary of the send call** ② the **starting point of the ledger row** — the current direct send does not go through `pending` and writes `delivered` straight away, but with all-queue **every send goes through `pending → delivered`.** Two observable consequences (spec §6 revision items): **transition rows that start with `delivered` become unreachable**, and **every send temporarily appears** in the argument-less `messages` query (= my unresolved items). Do not lump this as "ledger unchanged" — only the terminal vocabulary and meaning are unchanged.

**4. Ring the doorbell after loading.** The occasion for the flush lane to open the queue is created by the send itself. The "trapping" of context 1 above is **expected** to vanish structurally as the direct-send path disappears, but **that vanishing must be asserted by tests at implementation** (see §영향 unverified below).

## 거부한 대안

- **Keep the seat reservation (the 2026-07-31 implementation `76ea887` as it is)** — the order is guaranteed, but the burst is serialized so 100 concurrent sends become 1 `delivered` + 99 `pending`. That is, **the device built to protect `delivered` removes 99% of `delivered` in a burst** — the purpose and the cost diverge. Moreover direct send, seat and joining decisions referenced each other and the code kept swelling (the substance of the user's remark).
- **Remove only the seat reservation and close the isolation separately** — a plan that gives up the order of concurrently arriving items. A burst of 100 all answers `delivered`, so the response meaning is the strongest. Reason for rejection = **the race window remains as it is.** It does not merely give up the order; it keeps carrying the window between decision and injection, and the devices that handle that window (joining decision · in-flight accounting) remain too. The user wanted simplification and this plan does not get simpler.
- **The sending thread finishes the flush synchronously and answers `delivered`** — a plan with a single lane that also keeps the response meaning. Reason for rejection = **the sender pays for someone else's backlog.** Detaching the flush from the sending thread is intended design (`with_flush_trigger` — so the batch write does not run on the MCP/HTTP request thread), and reverting it makes one sender take on the whole backlog of the recipient's pending letters in its own request time. **Lock discipline is not a reason for rejection** — a synchronous flush also runs outside the lock (the `ring_or_defer` and `request_flush` docs state that even the inline fallback is called outside the lock). If the basis is set there, the next session will wrongly design with "impossible because of the lock".
- **Add an asynchronous delivery receipt (XMPP XEP-0184 style — the send answers `pending` and a separate signal comes at actual delivery)** — it is the closest to industry practice and the proper way to revive `delivered`. **We are not doing it now; it is not discarded for being wrong** — if the need is actually observed, we add it non-destructively then (v2 candidate). If we put it in now, it widens the observation surface for a signal the consumer (the sending LLM) would not even use.

## 근거

- **User decision (2026-08-01)** — when asked whether the order guarantee was needed, the user directly proposed the all-queue plan as "큐에 적재하고 뺄 때 한꺼번에 빼면 되는 거 아님?", and after the explanation that the `failed` branches remain (typos and nonexistent recipients are still rejected immediately) confirmed it with "ㅇㅇ 그렇게 가자".
- **ADR-0121's rejection reason differed from the facts (load-bearing — the reason this ADR partially amends 0121).** 0121 rejected 「정통 FIFO — 새 편지를 큐 끝에 넣고 끝」 saying "the delivery of the new letter becomes tied to the parking retry loop", and at the time attached to that sentence *(the exact conditions under which self-healing fires are to be confirmed at implementation — unverified)*. The implementation session confirmed that **the adopted behavior is exactly tail-join + doorbell, and the doorbell existed before this slice** — that is, the premise of the rejection does not hold. This fact was recorded as "open" in the slice C handoff document, and since the body edit needed the user's approval it was only reported. **It is not a basis for overturning the decision; it is the confirmation that the obstacle that blocked overturning it never existed.**
- **Industry practice survey (`/research` light, 2026-08-01 — no adversarial review, confidence applied conservatively):** cases where a send call synchronously returns "actually delivered" are rare. The actor model (Erlang `!`, Akka `tell`) and brokers (RabbitMQ publish, Kafka `acks`) **give no confirmation at all, or tell only that this hop received it** — RabbitMQ publisher confirms and Kafka `acks` mean "the broker/replica received it", not consumer receipt (per official documentation, confidence certain). Where confirmation is given, it is a **separate asynchronous signal** (Akka `ask` future, XMPP XEP-0184 delivery receipts — separating server hop / device receipt / read into layers). The counterexample is AutoGen `send_message`, which **blocks** until the recipient handler's return value comes out (checked directly in the official documentation — `publish_message` of the same framework, in contrast, returns `None` immediately). **The shape "one synchronous call returns the two outcomes delivered/pending, distinguished by a tag" was not found within the survey scope** — meaning our current API was not at a common point.
  - **Survey limits (honest):** being the light tier, there was no adversarial review, and CrewAI, LangGraph, Letta, the OpenAI Agents SDK, MCP agent messaging, Matrix and Signal are uncovered for exceeding the budget. The Google A2A item is based on an explainer article rather than the official spec text, so its confidence is one notch lower. **The "was not found" above is not an absence proof but a statement that includes the unexplored.**
- **The judgment that the practical impact of losing the response meaning is small** — there is **no path along which the sending agent would act differently** on seeing `delivered`/`pending` (both mean "accepted" and nothing is lost). Typos and nonexistent recipients, on the other hand, come back immediately as `failed`, so mistake detection is kept.

## 영향 / 불변식

- **There is one send path.** If **the send decision path calls `DeliveryPort::inject` directly**, this decision is violated — injection is owned by the flush lane. **Exception (do not remove):** in assemblies where the doorbell trigger is not wired (the experiment bin · kernel unit tests), **every branch that runs the flush synchronously on the sending thread** is not a violation — this includes not only the trigger-absent branch of `request_flush` but also the **`deferred_inline` drain** (the path where, after the response is fixed, the sending thread itself empties the doorbell that the send hung with `ring_or_defer`). With all-queue, this drain becomes the main fallback execution point of the kernel unit tests, so do not narrow the exception to one specific function. That is not direct send but *running the flush lane synchronously on the spot*, and it is the means by which unit tests obtain determinism (the `with_flush_trigger` doc makes the fallback an explicit contract). If it is misread as "sending-thread inject" and removed, the kernel test harness becomes nondeterministic.
- **The order is the loading order.** The order in which things entered the queue inside the lock is the delivery order. **What is forbidden is the reintroduction of `직발송 좌석 예약` and `직발송 합류 판정`** — if those two became necessary, it is a sign that the send path split in two.
  - **★Invariant (do not remove) — the duplicate-entry guard inside the flush lane is not the forbidden target above but a premise of this decision.★** If that recipient's in-flight remains, the flush entry does not drain but withdraws, leaving the doorbell hung as deferred (`service.rs` — `in_flight_len(recipient) > 0` → `deferred_flush` marker → break. The code comment states the reason: draining now would let later items be injected ahead of the remainder of the in-progress batch, causing **order inversion**). **Since all-queue made every message pass through the flush lane, this guard is the device that makes "delivery order = loading order" hold** — if it is removed on seeing only the name "in-flight accounting", the order guarantee this ADR meant to buy disappears itself. (Implementation contract = the spec §5 invariant "the one holding the receipt repays the deferred wake-up" · §7 acceptance criterion ②.)
- **The 4 `failed` kinds keep being decided before queue loading** — no such name · ambiguous same name · mailbox exceeded · **reply contract exceeded (`REQUEST_CAPACITY`)**. If these are absorbed into `pending`, the sender never learns of a typo, and in the case of `REQUEST_CAPACITY` **an untracked request gets delivered** (the rule that nothing is delivered to a recipient whose contract could not be opened collapses — canonical = spec §3 item 5. §5 merely references that rule as the basis for partial progress, so do not look for it there). Do not shorten it to "3 kinds" (ADR-0111 partial progress and per-recipient failure rows hang on this).
- **Ledger ↔ response vocabulary are different axes.** The ledger keeps writing `delivered` at the actual injection moment (ADR-0104). If it is deleted from the ledger because it vanished from the response, delivery observation collapses.
- **Skipping the idle gate stays** (ADR-0116 decision 7) — not asking the class without a turn signal about busy is unchanged. **But what this ADR changes is not only "who injects" — it also moves "who decides":** the send entry no longer consults the busy gate or the earlier parked items, and that decision **moves to the flush lane alone** (the entry looks only at the identity axis = the 4 `failed` kinds). The discipline of defining the gate predicate in one place is unchanged, but that "one place" is the flush lane. **What is skipped is the decision *location*, not the idle gate itself.**
- **Tests that flip (normal — the decision changed):** the kernel tests that assert that with an empty queue + idle it is injected immediately and answers `delivered` (the `empty_queue_idle_send_still_injects_directly` · `idle_recipient_injects_immediately` · `deliver_ok_marks_delivered_and_injects_wrapped` family) and the seat-reservation assertions (`a_direct_injection_holds_a_seat_so_a_concurrent_send_cannot_overtake_it` etc.) change to the new contract. **It must be a move (replaced by a different assertion), not a weakening (deleting the assertion)** — handling it by deletion loses the seal.
- **Unverified (must be asserted at implementation):** ① whether the "failed injection trapped in the queue" of context 1 really vanishes with the abolition of direct send — pin down by tests the causality of re-parking and doorbell for failures inside the flush lane. ② whether the doorbell always rings right after loading (if a branch that does not ring remains, that letter is trapped until the next occasion). ③ whether the flush batch creates response latency in a burst (the send call only goes up to loading, so no impact is expected).
